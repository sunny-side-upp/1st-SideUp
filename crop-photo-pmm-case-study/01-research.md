# Research Notes

## What the public product story says

Crop.photo describes a workflow that can:

- bulk crop, resize, align, enhance and clean product images
- generate studio / lifestyle / on-model visuals
- create banners and short PDP videos
- save reusable Recipes
- export for retailer specifications
- work with Shopify
- expose APIs for broader ecommerce workflows

These capabilities are visible in Crop.photo's current public product pages and Shopify listing.

## Competitive framing

I would not lead with:

> "We're another AI image generator."

The category already contains broad creative AI tools and specialized image products.

I would lead with:

> **"The visual production workflow for ecommerce catalogs."**

Then differentiate on the workflow:

**Source image → recipe → bulk processing → channel-ready output → repeatability**

## Buyer problem

The buyer does not necessarily wake up wanting "AI."

They may be experiencing:

- inconsistent product imagery
- repetitive retouching
- different marketplace specifications
- slow catalog updates
- expensive photoshoots
- creative teams becoming production bottlenecks

The PMM job is to connect the product to that operational pain.

## Validation plan

Before treating the positioning as fact, I would interview:

- 5 ecommerce leaders
- 5 creative / content operations leads
- 5 digital merchandisers
- 3 Shopify Plus operators

I would ask:

1. What happens when a new SKU enters the catalog?
2. How many visual variants are created?
3. Which edits are repeated?
4. Who approves imagery?
5. What causes rework?
6. Which marketplace requirements cause the most friction?
7. What would make an AI-generated image unacceptable?
8. Would a personalized visual audit be useful?

## Important distinction

This is a **portfolio hypothesis**, not an assertion that Crop.photo currently has this funnel.

That distinction is intentional: a PMM should be able to separate:

**Observed product fact → customer hypothesis → proposed strategy → experiment → evidence.**
