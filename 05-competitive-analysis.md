# 05 — Competitive Analysis

## Competitive set

| Alternative | Core strength | Strategic implication |
|---|---|---|
| Photoroom | E-commerce-focused visual production, editing, batch workflows and API | Strong direct competitor |
| Claid | Ecommerce image automation, APIs and custom pipelines | Strong workflow/API competitor |
| Adobe Firefly | Broad creative ecosystem + generative editing | Powerful horizontal incumbent |
| Internal creative workflow | Existing brand knowledge and control | Highest switching resistance |
| Agencies / freelancers | Human art direction | Alternative for premium campaigns |

## Public evidence

Photoroom currently describes AI product photography, batch processing, Brand Kit, marketplace workflows and APIs. Claid describes ecommerce automation, product scenes, APIs, image enhancement and custom image pipelines. Adobe Firefly describes AI product shots with control over composition, lighting and backgrounds.

## Competitive map

### Axis 1: Creative breadth
Low → High

### Axis 2: Workflow specialization
Low → High

**Hypothesis:** FrameFlow should compete toward the high-specialization end rather than trying to out-feature Adobe.

## Battlecard

### If competitor = generic creative suite

**Our angle:**  
"You can create almost anything. We are optimized for repeating one high-volume workflow."

### If competitor = image editor

**Our angle:**  
"The question isn't whether you can edit an image. It's whether you can operationalize that edit across thousands of products."

### If competitor = agency

**Our angle:**  
"Use human creative direction where it matters. Automate the repetitive production layer."

## What I would not claim

I would not claim FrameFlow is objectively faster, cheaper or higher quality without controlled tests.

A credible PMM should distinguish:

**Known competitor facts → hypothesis → proof required.**
